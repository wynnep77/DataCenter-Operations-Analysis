    How the simulation works
    
    
      
      
    • One shared model, refreshed every 2 seconds. All tabs read the same numbers, so a change on one tab shows up everywhere.
      • 
      
    • Load drives everything. More nodes or higher GPU use means more kW, which means more heat and higher flow, pump speed and temperatures.
      • 
      
    • Limits decide the outcome. Over a limit, heat goes to the room, the CRAC ramps, room temperature rises, and GPUs slow down to protect themselves.
      • 
      
    • PUE = total facility power ÷ IT power. With 11/18 °C facility water, chillers run for most of the year, so the annual PUE is about 1.25 with rear doors (about 1.30 at 80% liquid), compared with 1.60 for an air-cooled hall.
      • 
      
    • Status colours: green = within limits, amber = above 90% of a limit, red = over a limit or an active fault.
      • 
    
    
    
    
      
    Limit (modelled)ValueWhat happens when exceeded
    
      
    Rack power shelves165 kW (N+1)Rack cannot be powered at that load
    
      
    CL23 rear door150 kW per rackExtra heat spills to the room
    
      
    CDU capacity1,350 kW (N+1)Hall heat to water exceeds what one CDU can carry

    Detailed model notes · for technical audiences
    
    Data model
    
    
    A master tick fires every 2 seconds and advances one shared state object, after which only the active tab re-renders. On each tick every sensor advances by a bounded random walk added to a slow sinusoidal "IT load" cycle (the load index). Derived relationships:
    
    
      
      
    • Return temperatures = supply temperature + a load-driven ΔT.
      • 
      
    • Pump speeds, valve positions, flows and differential pressure track the load index.
      • 
      
    • GPU temperatures rise with the load index and with any out-of-band secondary supply.
      • 
      
    • Heat rejected (kW) = secondary flow × 4.18 × ΔT (Q = flow × 4.18 × ΔT).
      • 
    
    
  
    IT load scenario & impact model
    
    
    The IT Load Scenario bar under the tabs sets the load for the whole simulation. Auto cycle follows a sustained-training profile (GPU utilisation 85–99%). Manual lets you set utilisation from 10% to 100%. You can also set 1–8 nodes per rack; nodes 5–8 go into the reserved OU of the IR7000. The hall is modelled as eight racks with the same profile.
    
    
      
      
    • Node power = 4 kW + 16 kW × GPU utilisation, giving ≈18–20 kW sustained and 20 kW at 100%. Switches add 4 kW per rack. This rack-level envelope is kept from the original model so the load scaling is unchanged; it is a modelling assumption, not an XE9780L specification.
      • 
      
    • Server platforms: each DLC rack (Dell IR7050) holds either PowerEdge XE9780L nodes (2× Intel Xeon 6, 8× NVIDIA B300 1,100 W) or PowerEdge XE9785L nodes (2× AMD EPYC 9005, 8× AMD Instinct MI355X 1,400 W). Click a rack's platform tag in the Digital Twin to switch. XE9785L power scales the same envelope by GPU TDP: 4.5 kW + 20.4 kW × utilisation (≈24.9 kW at 100%), and its GPUs run ~2 °C hotter per unit of load. Default layout: Row A XE9780L, Row B XE9785L. A1 (Rack View) can be either.
      • 
      
    • Air-cooled racks (one by default at A5; add more with + Air rack in positions 5–12; not on the SFN loop). Build each one in the Air-cooled Rack Builder (Digital Twin) or in Rack View. Options: Dell IR5048 (48U) or IR5052 (52U); any quantity that fits (less 2U of switches) of PowerEdge XE9780 (10U, 8× B300 1,100 W or 8× B200 1,000 W, max 4 per rack), XE7740 (4U, 8× double-wide 600 W or 16× single-wide 75 W PCIe GPUs) and R7725 (2U, 2× AMD EPYC 9005: CPU only, 2× double-wide 450 W or 6× single-wide 75 W GPUs). Presets: Mixed (1 / 2 / 4), Training, Inference, CPU and Empty. Every server uses the same rule as the DLC nodes so platforms stay comparable: power = idle + (1.82 × total GPU TDP + base) × utilisation, where base = 1.5 kW of fans (XE9780) or 1.8 kW of CPUs (R7725), idle = 4 / 1.5 / 0.5 kW, plus 1.5 kW of switches. All heat goes to room air (CRAC) unless the rack's own rear door is fitted. GPU temperature follows the room inlet temperature, so air racks throttle first when the room overheats. Rack limit 80 kW, from Dell positioning guidance for 8-way air-cooled racks.
      • 
      
    • Digital Twin hall configuration: 1–24 racks (up to 12 per row), added or removed one at a time, alternating rows A and B, each with 1–8 XE9780L or XE9785L nodes (air racks have their own server mix) at the same GPU utilisation. A1 is the reference rack (Rack View, Live Console, failure scenarios). Other racks use a steady-state estimate of the same thermal model for GPU temperature and throttling. Hall IT, heat to water, heat spilled above CL23 (which drives the CRAC response), CDU N+1 load, BMS branches R01–R08 and Sustainability figures all sum the configured racks. Racks removed from A1–A4 / B1–B4 show their BMS branch as No rack.
      • 
      
    • Rack View GPU node detail: per-GPU power = GPU TDP (1,100 W B300 or 1,400 W MI355X) × (0.12 + 0.88 × utilisation), reduced by any throttling; per-GPU temperatures use fixed offsets from the node peak (GPU7 is the hottest). The NVLink domain is 8 GPUs per node, so 4 nodes = 32 GPUs per rack.
      • 
      
    • As load rises, secondary flow and CDU pump speed scale with load0.6 and secondary ΔT with load0.4, so flow × ΔT tracks heat. Differential pressure, primary flow (capped at 1.25× nominal) and primary return temperature rise with load.
      • 
      
    • Rear doors fitted (default): cold plates plus the CL23 rear door capture 100% of rack heat to water. Heat above the CL23 150 kW ceiling spills into the room. No rear doors: only the cold-plate share (slider, default 80%) goes to water, the remainder is rejected to room air, and the rack limit becomes the 165 kW power shelves. The CRAC units ramp up (output up to 118 kW for two units). Once their capacity is exceeded, room temperature climbs.
      • 
      
    • GPU temperature rises with utilisation, secondary supply temperature, spilled heat and room temperature. Above a modelled 88 °C throttle point, node power is capped by up to 30%, which lowers the compute actually delivered.
      • 
      
    • Limits tracked: power shelves 165 kW usable (5 + 1 N+1), CL23 150 kW, CDU N+1 1,350 kW per unit (modelled XDU1350-class rating), CDU pumps 100%, GPU 88 °C, room 27 °C. The BMS RDHX control valves open further with load and can saturate at 100%.
      • 
      
    • These capacity and throttle figures are modelling assumptions for enablement, not product specifications.
      • 
    
    
  
    PUE model — 11 °C / 18 °C chilled water
    
    
    The primary (facility) loop is designed for 11 °C supply / 18 °C return. At this temperature the heat cannot be rejected to outdoor air for most of the year without compressors, so a near-1.1 PUE is not achievable. The Sustainability tab therefore models an air-cooled free-cooling chiller plant:
    
    
      
      
    • Free cooling: the coil leaving temperature is outdoor air + 5 K. Full free cooling below ≈6 °C, partial up to ≈13 °C, none above.
      • 
      
    • Chiller: COP = 0.45 × Carnot with 8 °C evaporating and outdoor + 15 °C condensing, capped at 5.5. That gives ≈5.5 in mild weather, ≈4.0 at 25 °C and ≈3.4 at 30 °C.
      • 
      
    • Fixed overheads per kW IT: dry-cooler/condenser fans 3.5%, primary CHW pumps 2.5%, CDU pumps 1.2%, rear-door fans 0.8% (liquid share). CRAC 42% (air share). UPS and distribution losses 6.5%, lighting and controls 1%.
      • 
      
    • Result at 80% liquid: live PUE ≈1.22 in winter free cooling, ≈1.31 at 10 °C, ≈1.39 at 20 °C and ≈1.46 at 30 °C. Over an Irish midlands climate the annualised PUE is ≈1.30 (≈1.25 at 100% liquid). This sits below the 1.5–2.5 typical of chiller-based air-cooled halls.
      • 
      
    • Annual carbon, cost and energy-saved figures use the annualised PUE. Live PUE and the trend chart use the outdoor-temperature slider.
      • 
      
    • Raising the facility water temperature (e.g. ≥ 20 °C for rear doors, 32 °C for CDUs) would extend free cooling and is the main route to a lower PUE.
      • 
    
    
  
    BMS Monitoring tab
    
    
    Layout modelled on a BMS schematic capture of the CW distribution header: flow (green) and return (blue) headers with pressure sensors PS-01–PS-04 and temperature sensors TS-01–TS-04, CDU-001 and CDU-002, a room sensor, the leak detection panel, and eight rack / rear-door branch pairs (R01–R08, RDHX 1–8), each with control-valve position, branch flow and isolation-valve state.
    
    
      
      
    • Header temperatures track the simulated secondary supply (TS-01 ≈ Sec Supply T2 − 0.5 °C), so a console cooling fault shows up on the BMS too.
      • 
      
    • Branch flows and valve positions start from the values in the capture (hydraulic points, which stay similar with the plant at low load) and drift with the load index; RDHX control valves drive toward 100% during a cooling fault.
      • 
      
    • R01 is the IR7000 rack shown in Rack View, so failure scenarios appear on the R01 / RDHX 1 branches and on the leak panel.
      • 
      
    • The room sensor follows the computer-room model used by the CRAC panel (≈22.5 °C in normal operation). The capture's 20.3 °C was taken with the plant off for maintenance.
      • 
      
    • CDU-001 is the duty unit shown on the Live Console; CDU-002 (N+1 assist) is derived from the same state with small fixed offsets.

    • CRAC Units sub-tab (modelled on the BMS CRAC screen capture): CRAC 09 and CRAC 10, lead CRAC 10 by default. The lead unit runs and the standby starts when the room needs more than one unit (59 kW each). Valve feedback follows the command after ≈4 s. If a running unit has no chilled water for 6 s (valves closed in O/R) it latches a Run Fail. A failed or leaking lead triggers auto changeover. A leak closes that unit's valves. Room cooling capacity in the whole simulation is 59 kW per available unit, so losing a unit raises room temperature everywhere. Setpoints are 24 °C / 50 %RH; return-air-high alarms above setpoint + 3 °C or 60 %RH. With Modbus comms lost, the values read 0.0, as on the real BMS. The CRAC capture shows this state because it was taken during maintenance; in the simulation both units are in service by default.
